# Dracula: the I6 story outline

Bob's source for the story, in our words (DEC-739). It summarises his research note on the 1983 module *I6: Ravenloft*, which stays outside this public repo. **Names and locations will change, and any artwork is only inspiration** (Bob, 2026-11-05). Names below are the module's, used only to point at the source.

## The tragedy behind it
An ageing lord wants a woman who loves his younger brother. He blames his age, makes a pact with death and murders the brother on the wedding day. She throws herself from the castle rather than accept him. He becomes a vampire: he escapes death and never gets the love he wanted. Centuries later a burgomaster's adopted daughter has her face, and he turns on her. **She did not choose him.** His tragedy is trying to overcome someone's wishes by murder and power, not grief.

## How the story unfolds
| Phase | What happens | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| The invitation | A letter, supposedly from the burgomaster, draws the heroes in | Looks like a rescue |
| The trap | Magic fog stops them leaving the valley | Facing the evil becomes necessary |
| The village | The burgomaster is already dead; his son asks them to protect his sister | Makes the danger personal |
| The reading | A fortune teller gives clues to the objects and to the vampire | Direction without a fixed route |
| The castle | They search for tools and answers against him, his servants and the castle | Exploration against an active foe |
| The resolution | Destroying him ends it and lifts the fog | A definite win, not an endless cycle |

Heroes may take the castle and its contents in different orders. The daughter insists on going with them to the castle.

## The reading (the story engine)
Each game, cards decide where three objects are, where the vampire is, and which goal he pursues.

| Object | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| The Tome | His own history, and useful information |
| The Holy Symbol | Supernatural leverage against him |
| The Sunsword | A weapon made to face him |

Heroes get clues, pick destinations and improve their position before the final fight, rather than searching every room for a boss.

## His four possible goals (one per game)
1. **Replace a hero:** turn one into a vampire and take their identity.
2. **Win the daughter's love:** manipulate a hero into attacking her, then rescue her.
3. **Make a sphere of darkness:** chase a black opal he believes the heroes carry.
4. **Destroy the Sunsword** before it can be used on him.

Keep apart his lasting character (pride, jealousy, predation) and the goal the cards gave him.

## The villain: an opponent, not a final room
He is a vampire and a strong spellcaster, played as clever and self-preserving.
- **He picks his moments:** spies report where the heroes are, and he attacks when it suits him.
- **He withdraws:** a lost skirmish need not be his last stand.
- **His acts serve his goal:** an attack may take an object, manipulate a hero or wear them down rather than kill.

Heroes meet his influence, survive him, learn something and face him again.

## Other threats and inhabitants
| Threat | Role | Why it works |
|---|---|---|
| Witches | Spellcasters who ambush through secret passages | They use the building |
| Zombie servants | Expendable undead in his attacks | His reach through agents |
| Wolves and bats | Harass heroes, outside the castle too | The land is unsafe as well |
| Guardian portrait | A picture that hypnotises | Decoration becomes the threat |
| Guardian of Sorrow | A hanging heart in a tower; the tower pitches and halberds attack | The building is the enemy |
| Wights in the crypts | The swap trap below | Danger plus apparent betrayal |

Not everyone is an enemy. His accountant will talk; others may be innocent, compromised or falsely friendly, so heroes investigate people instead of fighting everyone.

## Set pieces
1. **Defending the house:** heroes may guard the daughter in the village. He harasses them at night with wolves and bats. He cannot enter uninvited, so he tries to charm someone into letting him in. The danger is whether everyone inside stays trustworthy.
2. **The reading:** exposition as an event, with enough to plan and not enough to end discovery. (Leave out the original's ethnic stereotypes of travelling fortune tellers; keep the card-reading structure.)
3. **The banquet and the organ:** a feast and the host at the organ. He is an illusion; he vanishes, the doors close and statues attack. He controls the meeting: a performance before a fair fight.
4. **The Guardian of Sorrow:** fighting a haunted place, not a creature in one.
5. **The flooded room:** zombies wait under the water; the room hides the enemy.
6. **The elevator:** a useful mechanism that crushes whoever stands in the wrong place; understanding it matters as much as surviving it.
7. **The wight swap:** near his tomb, a hero's body is swapped with a wight's, and the hero's gear stays behind. The others see their friend turned undead; the real hero is alone in a crypt with nothing. "Is that still our companion?"

## How it fits together
Fixed rooms, wandering checks (day, night and castle tables), deceptive rooms, traps, harmless people and truly empty spaces. It is not a chain of fights: quiet builds tension, suspects build doubt, traps break positions, and he exploits the weakness. Many routes between floors support that.

## Visual motifs (inspiration only)
- Hospitality hiding coercion: banquets, organs, portraits, sealed doors.
- Home under siege: shuttered houses and predators outside.
- A castle that seems alive: a hanging heart, shaking towers, weapons with no wielders.
- The past persisting: portraits, his journal, family tombs, ruined ceremonial halls.
- Uncertain identity: a false host, hypnotic images, a friend replaced.
- The vampire above his castle: the master of the place, not one creature inside it.

# Opening scene: The Lantern & Bell

Status: draft for reaction. Nothing here is decided.

**When:** All Hallows' Eve, dusk, a storm rolling in.
**Where:** The Lantern & Bell, the only inn in Vellmoor, at the crossroads under the castle.
**Why they're here:** Each hero received the same letter, sealed in black wax, from Father Oskar, Vellmoor's priest: *"Come before the eve of All Hallows. I have found where he sleeps. Tell no one."* None of them knows the others were invited.

Format notes: **[APP]** is what the web app shows or plays. **[TOWER]** is a tower cue. Narration is read by the narrator voice; dialog lines are voiced by the characters.

---

## Beat 1: The valley at dusk

[APP] Slow pan over a painted valley. Wind, distant wolves, a church bell ringing the hour. Music: low strings.

[TOWER] Lights off. Silent.

> **Narrator:** Vellmoor has buried more of its own this year than in the ten before it. The harvest rots in the fields because no one will work past sundown. Tonight is All Hallows' Eve, and the castle on the hill is lit for the first time in a generation.

[APP] The camera settles on the inn's window, warm amber light.

## Beat 2: The inn

[APP] Interior: low beams, a roaring fire, garlic braids over every window, a dozen nervous villagers. The innkeeper, **Hanne**, a stout woman with a cleaver tucked in her apron, polishes the same cup over and over.

> **Narrator:** Four strangers have come to Vellmoor today. The village has noticed all of them.

## Beat 3: The arrivals

Each hero enters in turn. The app shows a portrait, plays their entrance, and players read or hear their first line. Order is fixed so the scene flows; each player meets their character here.

**1. Tomas Varga** is already there, in the corner, back to the wall, his third tankard in front of him. The villagers give him a wide berth; some of them remember the livery.

> **Hanne:** Varga. I'd heard you were dead.
> **Tomas:** *(taps the axe haft three times)* Not for lack of trying, lass.

**2. Sister Agnes Hallow** comes in out of the rain, dripping, and goes straight to the fire. She studies the garlic over the windows.

> **Agnes:** You keep a fire burning all night, innkeeper. What are you afraid of?
> **Hanne:** Same as you, Sister, or you wouldn't have asked.

**3. Emeric Lutz** bursts through the door, soaked, his satchel clinking, and walks straight into a villager. Apologizes four times.

> **Emeric:** I'm looking for Father Oskar? I have a letter. That is, he sent me a letter. That is, I'm expected. I think.

The room goes quiet. Tomas looks up. Agnes turns from the fire. Both reach into their coats for the same black-sealed letter.

> **Tomas:** *(slowly)* So am I, lad.
> **Agnes:** It seems Father Oskar has been writing a great many letters.

**4. Wren Kessel** comes in last, with mud to the knees. She smells the air, finds the three of them with the black-sealed letters out on the table, and drops her own beside them.

> **Wren:** Father Oskar's not coming.
> **Emeric:** How do you — is he ill?
> **Wren:** Grave in the churchyard's been dug from the inside. His name's on the stone. He was buried this morning.

## Beat 4: Introductions

[APP] Interactive. The four portraits sit around the table. Each player taps their hero to deliver a short introduction (who they are, why they came, written from the backstories). The players' choices of follow-up questions open small reactions, for example:

- Agnes asks Tomas about his livery → *"I wore it. Then I stopped wearing it. That's all there is to say."*
- Tomas asks Emeric what's in the satchel → *"Reagents! Mostly reagents. One small bottle of something I really shouldn't open indoors."*
- Emeric asks Wren about the names on her bolts → she says nothing and takes the bolt back.
- Wren asks Agnes about the scar → *"Do you ask everyone about their wounds, child?"*

This is where players learn each hero's ability in play terms (the app shows a card next to each portrait).

## Beat 5: The letter

Laid side by side, the four letters show something more: each has a different line written in the margin in faded ink, in Father Oskar's hand.

- **Agnes's:** *"He keeps the faith of the dead."*
- **Tomas's:** *"He rests where the guard once stood."*
- **Wren's:** *"He lies beneath what was dug."*
- **Emeric's:** *"He sleeps in the name of the first."*

> **Emeric:** It's a riddle. He split it between us so no single one of us could — oh. Oh, that's clever. That's very clever.
> **Tomas:** It's a riddle that got a priest killed.

[APP] The four clues go into the party's journal: the first pieces of the true-coffin riddle.

## Beat 6: Midnight

[APP] The church bell begins to toll midnight. Music cuts out. The fire gutters.

[TOWER] The tower wakes: red lights rise slowly from the base to the top. The bell sound plays from the tower. A drum turns, grinding.

> **Narrator:** Up on the hill, the castle doors open.

[APP] A scream from outside. A villager staggers in from the yard, a hand pressed to his neck, and collapses.

[TOWER] The first skull drops: a villager has been turned. Wherever it lands, that building is under the Count's shadow from the start.

> **Wren:** *(loading the crossbow)* He knows we're here.
> **Agnes:** Then he's afraid of something. That's the first good news I've heard in ten years.
> **Tomas:** *(standing, axe in hand)* Hanne, bar the door behind us.
> **Emeric:** Behind — behind us? We're going *out*?

## Beat 7: First choice

[APP] The scene ends on the party's first decision, and play begins:

1. **Follow the screams** into the night (a first fight, teaches battle).
2. **Go to the churchyard** to see Father Oskar's open grave (a first clue, teaches searching).
3. **Hold the inn until dawn** (a safe start, but the clock advances and the Count gets a free turn).

> **Narrator:** Six nights until the Count's ritual is complete. The hunt begins.

---

## Notes for the design

- **Each hero's private tie is a hook the app can pay off later:** Agnes learning why she was spared, Tomas's niece, Wren's blank bolt, Emeric's cipher.
- **The four margin clues are the riddle's first layer.** Their answers point to buildings or crypts on the board: the chapel, the old guard post, the churchyard, the founder's tomb.
- **"Six nights" is a placeholder** for the day/night clock length.
- **The fixed entry order** lets one script work for any player count. With fewer players, unused heroes could still appear as NPCs who join later.

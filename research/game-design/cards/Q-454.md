Hammer's status check (item 1b): in P-006 a script that never splits and takes the nearest quest wins 83 to 97% at normal and 100% at easy. If a plain policy wins, the tension has to come from somewhere else.

**Parked with P-008 (bloodtower #475, parked to 1.1).** No 1.0 rule changes here.
- **Why no rule:** DEC-823 sets normal as a floor of about 80% for a plain party on purpose, so a first table wins most of its games. Choices are meant to matter at hard (a band of about 50 to 85%, DEC-830), and where a table plays the clock (splitting, guarding, spending).
- **1.0 evidence, no design work:** the first human playtest (P01) asks at the end of each game, per decision type, "did this change the result?" for guarding, spending a card, splitting, and the tower's skulls. That is observation, not a rule.
- **The experiment** is P-008: the same cells with a random, a plain and a careful policy, and a splitting policy at hard (paper playtest PT-06's lever). It waits with 1.1.
- **Answer when:** P-008 shows a careful policy beating a plain one by a clear margin at hard, or P01 finds nobody could name a decision that mattered.

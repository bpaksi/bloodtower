Bob picked "A quest ends the turn". P-006's script found the gap: Enter is free and a quest pick costs 1 move (MEC-035, MEC-036), so nothing stopped a group playing two or three quests close together on one day. P-001 to P-005 played about one a day, and this makes that the rule.

- **The rule:** when a quest pick resolves, the group's turn ends, whether it won, retreated or fell. Its moves left are lost, as at any turn's end (DEC-149).
- **Not affected:** other picks (rest, buy, talk, Cleanse) and walking still cost 1 move each, and a group may make several in a turn. A wild battle from an event comes at the day's end, so it is unchanged.
- **Why:** days stay a real cost, so the skull clock works. In the script, with P-005's party at Long, no limit wins 97.5% by about day 15. With the quest ending the turn it wins 71% by about day 22, near P-005's 63% by day 19.

**Claude's details, for Bob to check:** the rule has no `release` field, so it applies to 1.0 too, since the 1.0 tuning was probed that way. The 1.0 code needs checking against it. The step goes on MEC-035's railroad when that card is next edited.

Not picked: no limit, as written; and a battle ends the turn while a plain scene doesn't.

**Provisional (DEC-914, Gamewright, 2026-11-13):** picked from a script's win rates (P-006). It stays in force and is built as written; the first human 1.0 playtest checks it; it changes only with Bob.

**With Fortunate (DEC-913, Gamewright, 2026-11-13):** the quest pick ends the turn before Fortunate's free second pick; a group that wants both takes the quest second.
